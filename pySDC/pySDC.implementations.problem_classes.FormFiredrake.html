class FormFiredrake(V, mass, rhs, bcs=None, t=None, u_exact=None, solver_parameters=None, LHS_cache_size=12, stop_at_divergence=True, nullspace=None)[source]#

Bases: Problem

Generic problem given by UFL weak forms and solved with Firedrake, with the right-hand side treated implicitly.

The semi-discrete system is

\[M u' = R(u),\]

where \(M\) is assembled from the mass form \(m(u, v)\) and \(R(u)\) from the right-hand side form \(r(u, v)\), all of it treated implicitly (see FormFiredrakeIMEX for an explicit part, and total_rhs() for joining the parts of a split problem). Time enters only through the Firedrake Constant t, which is set to the requested time before every evaluation and solve, so forms, boundary values and the exact solution can all depend on it.

This class never inverts \(M\): eval_f returns the load vector \(R(u)\), so it must be used with the mass-matrix sweepers generic_implicit_mass and imex_1st_order_mass.

Dual vectors. \(R(u)\), \(M u\), the right-hand sides of the solves and the sweeper’s residual are load vectors, which live in the dual space. They are stored as Cofunction objects (dtype_f is firedrake_dual_mesh), so adding one to a solution by mistake raises, and their abs is the norm dual to the \(L^2\) norm, which the residual reports.

Dirichlet boundary conditions. Nothing is assembled with bcs, because Firedrake zeros boundary rows and columns then, while the collocation problem needs the full \(M u_0\) and \(R(u)\) on the rows of the interior nodes. The boundary values are imposed only by the solves, where Firedrake replaces the boundary rows by \(u = g(t)\). The boundary rows of the sweeper’s residual therefore carry no equation; fix_residual zeros them (the sweepers call it because fix_bc_for_residual is set).

Several levels. MLSDC and PFASST work with base_transfer_mass and MeshToMeshFiredrakeHierarchy on the meshes of a Firedrake MeshHierarchy. Give V, bcs and u_exact per level, as lists in the problem parameters, since each lives on its level’s mesh. On coarse levels, the solves impose the boundary values of the restricted fine solution instead of the data (use_restricted_boundary_values), so the coarse correction is zero on the boundary.

Parameters:

  • V (firedrake.FunctionSpace) – Function space of the solution

  • mass (callable) – Mass form \(m(u, v)\)

  • rhs (callable) – Right-hand side form \(r(u, v)\), may be nonlinear in \(u\)

  • bcs (tuple of firedrake.DirichletBC, optional) – Boundary conditions imposed in the solves. Their values may depend on t, and they may act on a sub-space, such as one field of a mixed space or one component of a vector space. Pass a tuple, not a list: pySDC reads lists in the problem parameters as one entry per level.

  • t (firedrake.Constant, optional) – The time the forms depend on. A new one is made if none is given.

  • u_exact (UFL expression, optional) – Exact solution in terms of t and a spatial coordinate. Needed for u_exact.

  • solver_parameters (dict, optional) – PETSc options for the solves, on top of Firedrake’s defaults (Newton with a direct linear solver). If the Jacobian of the implicit system does not contain the unknown, i.e. the implicit part is linear, one linear solve replaces Newton’s method (LINEAR); if it does not contain t either, its assembly and factorization are kept from one solve to the next (CONSTANT_JACOBIAN). Options given here take precedence, e.g. {'snes_lag_jacobian': 1} if the Jacobian depends on a coefficient that is changed between solves.

  • LHS_cache_size (int, optional) – Number of solvers kept, one per factor

  • stop_at_divergence (bool, optional) – Raise if a solve does not converge, instead of logging it

  • nullspace (firedrake.VectorSpaceBasis or firedrake.MixedVectorSpaceBasis, optional) – Null space of the implicit systems, e.g. the constants of a pressure or stream function with no Dirichlet condition. Passed on to the solves; a direct solver then needs null pivot detection, such as MUMPS with 'mat_mumps_icntl_24': 1.

solve_system(rhs, factor, u0, t)[source]#

Solve \(M u - \text{factor}\, R(u) = \text{rhs}\) with the boundary conditions at time t.

The Jacobian comes from ufl.derivative. One Firedrake NonlinearVariationalSolver is set up per factor and cached; the oldest one is evicted once more than LHS_cache_size are around.

Parameters:

  • rhs (firedrake_dual_mesh) – Right-hand side, a load vector

  • factor (float) – Prefactor of the implicit right-hand side, e.g. the node-to-node step size

  • u0 (dtype_u) – Initial guess
