implementations.transfer_classes.BaseTransferDelta module¶

Space-time transfer for the delta-form hierarchy.

BaseTransfer makes a coarse level rebuild its own residual from \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) coarse data and recovers the coarse-grid correction as \(u_G - u_G^{\mathrm{old}}\). Both have exact algebraic replacements – \(\varepsilon_G = R\varepsilon_F\), which is what the FAS \(\tau\) is for, and the correction is the sum of the sweep’s own increments – and this transfer uses those instead. It is what decides whether a level rebuilds its residual or is handed one: the delta-form sweepers do both, and this is what hands one down.

The FAS \(\tau\) is substituted, not discarded. Writing \(\tau = R(\Delta t\,Q_F f_F) - \Delta t\,Q_G f_G\) and putting it into the coarse residual \(\Delta t (Q_G f_G) + u_G[0] - u_G[m] + \tau\) cancels the \(\Delta t\,Q_G f_G\) terms identically and leaves \(R\varepsilon_F\). So a coarse level handed that residual is solving precisely the FAS-corrected problem – and must not also add \(\tau\), which would count it twice. What is skipped is materialising \(\tau\) as an array, since the quantity it exists to produce arrives directly.

Identical to BaseTransfer up to round-off at backend precision, verified for two, three and four levels and for PFASST – and against a control with \(\tau\) genuinely zeroed, which does not converge at all. It is also slightly cheaper, because nothing then reads \(\tau\) except compute_end_point() in the quadrature-update case: see delta_transfer.restrict().

What the reformulation buys is that every coarse-level quantity becomes proportional to the fine residual rather than to \(|u|\). A coarse level is a preconditioner whose returned correction tends to zero, so once nothing on it is rebuilt out of \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) state, its arithmetic error is proportional to that correction rather than to the solution – which is what lets it run below backend precision without capping the accuracy of the iteration.

Bases: BaseTransfer

Space-time transfer that passes a residual down and an accumulated correction up.

Drop-in for BaseTransfer, and identical to it up to round-off when every level is at backend precision. Falls back to the stock prolongation when the coarse level has banked no corrections, so a hierarchy mixing delta-form and stock sweepers still runs.

coarse_reads_tau()[source]¶

Whether the coarse level reads the FAS \(\tau\) at all.

The sweep does not – it is handed the restricted fine residual, which is what \(\tau\) exists to produce – and neither does DeltaFormMixin.compute_residual(). The one remaining reader is compute_end_point(), and only when the end point comes from the quadrature update rather than a copy of the last node. When nothing reads it, building it is a coarse integrate() and a restriction spent on a quantity that is then discarded.

Returns:

Whether \(\tau\) has to be built.

Return type:

bool

prolong()[source]¶

Add the coarse level’s accumulated correction to the fine level.

If the fine level is itself a coarse level of something above it, its residual and its own accumulated correction are advanced by the same update, so neither has to be rebuilt from \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) state later in the V-cycle.
